## Source and distinction review

Sources were checked on 30 September using Parallel Search and direct primary-source reads. Parallel session: session_818797be9af621a036fd5b8c6370e3be. Article JSON files contain the public source links.

- `words-that-mean-their-own-opposite`: Merriam-Webster's contronym explainer and the dictionary entries for dust and fast verify both senses. Original sentence pairs and a reveal exercise distinguish this from the existing Connections strategy guide. No invented etymology or copied dictionary examples.
- `can-a-wrong-guess-help-you-learn`: Richland, Kornell and Kao (2009), author-hosted full paper, DOI 10.1037/a0016496. Five experiments, an essay about vision, pretesting followed by study, and benefits for initially failed items. Effects on untested material varied. The article does not claim that errors alone teach, that all learning benefits, or that BrainiLab has proven effects. Its sequence exercise is explicitly illustrative, not a replication or efficacy test. This complements the existing after-learning retrieval guide.
- `why-tennis-balls-are-yellow`: ITF's history PDF verifies black/white predecessors, television visibility and the 1972 rule change. Wimbledon's own museum learning PDF independently confirms its 1986 adoption. No assertion of a universal 1972 switch or current ban on white balls. Original archive-caption exercise distinguishes colour from exact dating.
- `why-britain-skipped-eleven-days-in-1752`: the Calendar (New Style) Act, section 1, verifies 2 September followed by 14 September and eleven omitted labels. Royal Museums Greenwich explains the Julian/Gregorian change and leap-year context. No vanished elapsed time, worldwide simultaneous adoption or unsupported riot story. This complements, rather than repeats, the leap-year and century articles.
- `how-hook-and-loop-fasteners-work`: Smithsonian Lemelson Center and NIHF verify burr inspiration, matching hooks and loops, manufacturing trials and the 1955 patent. Sources disagree about the year of the inspiring walk, so no year is given. Brand and generic mechanism are distinguished. Original inspection exercise compares mechanical fastening with Post-it adhesive.
- `why-ballpoint-pens-have-a-tiny-ball`: NIHF verifies Bíró's modern successful design, thick ink problem, socket and rotating-ball transfer. BIC's dated 2011 product description supports the specifically attributed Cristal material and point/line measurements. No claim that Bíró held the earliest ballpoint patent, that every pen uses tungsten carbide, or that point diameter equals line width. Original sketch exercise explains the mechanism.
