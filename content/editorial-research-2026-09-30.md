## Priority queue

1. **How can a word mean its own opposite?** — Words & puzzles (currently 1 article). Target intent: understand contronyms. Build around three original pairs of sentences, with a reveal explaining which meaning fits. Distinct from the existing Connections strategy guide: this is about opposing meanings, not arbitrary category connections. Link to that guide and Connections. Future cluster: Word meanings and connections. [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/wordplay/words-own-opposites) explains contronyms, including fast and cleave. Verify each chosen sense in its dictionary entry; do not imply all examples have the same etymology.

2. **Can a wrong guess help you remember the answer?** — Learning (1). Target intent: understand pretesting. Give one original question, ask for a guess, then explain the answer and invite later recall. Focus on guessing before study, distinct from the existing post-quiz retrieval guide. Future cluster: Remembering what you learn. [Richland, Kornell & Kao, 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19751074/) reports five experiments; [Kornell, Hays & Bjork, 2009](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2009-09620-017) compares attempted answers with reading answers. Read methods and limitations before drafting; incorrect guesses without corrective information are not the intervention. Do not claim BrainiLab itself has proven cognitive benefits.

3. **Why did tennis balls turn yellow?** — Sports (2). Target intent: tennis-ball colour history. Explain visibility on television, distinguish the ITF rule change in 1972 from Wimbledon's adoption in 1986. Add a short timeline and an original recall question. Link to the tennis-scoring guide and Sports game. Future cluster: Understanding a tennis match. [ITF history of tennis balls](https://www.itftennis.com/media/2280/balls-history-of-tennis-balls.pdf) directly supports both dates. Avoid unsupported claims that a single person invented the change, or that white balls became universally illegal.

Before drafting, verify the exact date transition against the Act or an archival source. Do not repeat the riots story as established fact.
