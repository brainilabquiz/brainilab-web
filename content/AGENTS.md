# Article publication requirements

Writing workflow: use the shared review in `lib/article-writing.js` and the private brief/review commands documented in `docs/article-writing-workflow.md`. Keep dated demand evidence, claim-level source checks, English and cover review, and mobile/desktop evidence beside the private draft. Never store briefs or captures in public content directories. Draft review and publication review are distinct; suggestions are not quotas or an SEO score, and a manual reviewed label is not independent factual verification. Preserve the established guarded publisher and its revision/history checks.

These requirements reflect the user's explicit instruction of 11 October 2026: no new article may be published without following all the editorial, SEO, AEO and GEO guidelines. They apply to articles, Academy lessons and editorial learning paths.

Use the saved Growth report as dated context when available: `brief ... --growth /private/path/growth-report.json` creates a minimised private, hash-bound link/evidence plan. Never copy the full report, outreach identities or correspondence into public content. Keep exact URLs and each source's dates; do not add overlapping windows, treat missing rows as zero, or treat a distribution receipt or outgoing citation as a verified backlink. New version 2 briefs require central-answer attribution and actual link checks from the last 24 hours. See the writing workflow for the fields and backward-compatible review rules. This does not authorise new tracking, outreach, paid research or public content changes by itself.

For public English, also follow PUBLIC_COPY.md in this directory. The user requested clear, easy English and accurate, inviting headlines on 12 October 2026. Keep account invitations voluntary and separate from any future newsletter subscription.

Before drafting, read current published content to avoid overlapping search intent. Research the candidate keywords using first-party Search Console queries when available, or dated third-party demand estimates with their market and language. Check search-result intent and competing pages. Reuse research from the previous 30 days where applicable. Never invent volume, difficulty, rankings or traffic. Unknown data is unknown, not zero. If suitable demand evidence is unavailable, keep the article in draft rather than publishing it as researched.

Write original, entertaining English for readers. Explain the actual question near the start and support the answer with specific examples. Use relevant searched terms naturally in the title, description, headings or text; do not force unrelated trending terms or a quota of keywords into an article. Public copy must not expose internal acquisition, SEO, monetisation or retention objectives. AEO and GEO mean clear, attributable explanations that can be understood and cited; they do not guarantee AI mentions or require artificial FAQ blocks.

Open and check reliable primary sources for factual claims, including the exact detail being cited. Cite the appropriate page, not a search-results page. Check calculations, word examples, answer keys and uncertainty. Keep a dated research and source record outside the public article body. Do not claim a blocked page was read successfully.

Make each article comfortable to read: short paragraphs, a few meaningful headings, no duplicated introduction, large resource widgets or mandatory exercises. Quizzes and answers, when useful, remain optional and folded. Every article needs its own relevant original cover, truthful credit, meaningful alternative text and lightweight 960/480/240 WebP derivatives. Inspect the art, counts, lettering and thumbnail crops.

Give every piece a clear place in Learn and, where relevant, Academy. Preserve existing lesson order, identifiers and quiz versions when extending a course. Add contextual internal links, an appropriate game and a restrained invitation to play. Do not add a forced account wall to reading. Use the existing renderer for author, Article schema, canonical URL, social metadata, RSS and sitemap; check the generated and live result rather than assuming content JSON publishes it.

Before publishing, complete and save a review of demand, intent, novelty, sources, examples, readable layout, metadata/schema, links, covers and mobile appearance. Run the existing article/quiz/course validators, check full-correct and full-wrong quiz scoring, and inspect mobile and desktop. Deploy and verify cover assets first, then use the established editorial/public CMS publication workflow with revision checks and history. Do not impersonate an admin JWT. Never overwrite concurrent editorial work or publish unrelated dirty files.

After publication, verify the article, its course/category links, source attribution, images, canonical URL, schema, RSS and sitemap on the live domain. Report only verified outcomes: being published and indexable is different from being indexed, ranking, attracting visits or producing accounts. Save progress and evidence so these requirements continue in later sessions.

Category balance (user instruction, 16 October 2026): before selecting new articles or Academy paths, audit current published counts and distinguish regular articles from Academy lessons. Numbers & logic is already the largest category because it has four courses; do not keep expanding it by default. Prioritise underrepresented categories such as Sports, History, Geography, Words & puzzles, Learning, Curiosity, Everyday inventions and Science & nature when a distinct reader need and search evidence support the topic. Grow Getting started only to fill a useful missing guide. Preserve article quality and course coherence: do not create filler, duplicate intent, move articles to misleading categories, or impose equal counts for their own sake. A specific user request or a documented distinct opportunity can justify a new maths article. Keep this balance in the editorial plan without exposing internal priorities in public copy.

Demand evidence quality (17 October 2026): record the actual relevant query or question, market, language, date and evidence before drafting. Search Console page impressions do not reveal that page’s queries unless the page filter was applied. A normalised Google Trends index, a single news spike, a broad topic with shopping intent or an unrelated trending seed does not establish strong demand for a specific English article. Do not describe these as the most searched terms or invent monthly volumes. Prefer a distinct useful question supported by relevant evidence over filling a category. When evidence is too weak to justify a new article, retain the proposal as a draft. For an existing useful article with incomplete demand evidence, record the gap, preserve its URL and assess real queries after sufficient data accumulates; do not remove it or add noindex just because a report omits it. Use targeted evidence-based revisions, not blanket keyword stuffing or repetitive rewrites.
