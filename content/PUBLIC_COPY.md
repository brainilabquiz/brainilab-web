# Public English

Write for someone who wants a pleasant read or a quick game. Use everyday English, short paragraphs and direct instructions. Keep British spelling in ordinary copy; retain official names, quoted wording and established search terms when appropriate.

Headlines should reveal the real subject and give a reason to be curious. Prefer one clear question or a concrete surprise. Keep the relevant search term naturally, but avoid vague hype, keyword lists, false promises or a “why” that the article never answers. Use sentence case, apart from names and abbreviations.

Explain one action at a time. Name the benefit before the implementation. Readers do not need database names, deployment instructions, internal acquisition goals or old launch dates in ordinary interface copy. Preserve material privacy, payment and scoring information, using plain language and optional folded details where suitable.

Keep instructions current: the main Daily maintains the streak, and one extra is optional. Historical scores and archived games retain their own rules. Do not describe retired interactive resources or give fixed lesson counts in shared recommendations.

Invite readers to create a free account where it helps them keep game or course progress. Make the invitation dismissible and open sign-up only after a click. Reading and playing remain available without registration. Creating an account is not consent to a newsletter: any future email subscription needs its own voluntary choice and a way to unsubscribe.

For language edits, preserve URLs, factual meaning, citations, artwork, answer keys, quiz versions and score calculations. A wording-only repair can clarify a quiz prompt without changing what it asks or how it is graded. Use the guarded editorial/public workflow and keep revision history. Confirm headline changes appear consistently in the page, library, metadata, schema, feed and course links.
